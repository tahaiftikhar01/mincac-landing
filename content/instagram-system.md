# Instagram Content System — Taha / MinCac

Built from the MinCac site copy (positioning, offers, proof, testimonials). When the Obsidian vault becomes readable, layer your own notes on top — the structure below won't change, only the raw material feeding it.

## 1. The positioning

LinkedIn rewards the company. Instagram rewards the person. Do not clone the MinCac feed onto Instagram — post as **Taha, the automation engineer**, with MinCac as the thing you happen to run.

**One-line positioning:**
> I build the systems that do the work nobody should be doing by hand — and I show you exactly how, for free.

The reason this works in 2026: everyone can generate a polished carousel now. Nobody can fake being the person who actually shipped a 22-node n8n workflow at 1am and can talk about the node that kept breaking. Your unfair advantage is not information — it is being *verifiably* the person who did the thing. So the camera stays on your face and the screen recording stays real, warts included.

**Three things every post should carry:**
1. Your face or your voice (not a stock B-roll voiceover).
2. A real artifact — a workflow canvas, a dashboard, a Slack notification firing, a client message.
3. A specific number. You have them: 80+ hours, 300 leads/month, 1 day → 5 minutes, 40% conversion lift.

## 2. Content pillars

Five pillars, rotated. Roughly the weekly mix in brackets.

### Pillar 1 — "Watch me build it" [2 posts/week]
Screen recording of an actual automation being assembled, your voice over it, face cam in the corner. 45–75 seconds. One automation, one outcome, no theory.

Straight from your existing builds:
- The Shopify + Meta Ads + DailyFulfill profitability system — three n8n automations feeding one Google Sheet.
- The audit bot at mincac.com/audit — 22 nodes, 5-minute discovery, scores the lead, routes hot ones to Calendly.
- The LinkedIn lead engine — scrape, enrich, personalize, log to HubSpot.
- The Instagram DM auto-responder — the meta joke writes itself: an automation that answers Instagram DMs, posted on Instagram. Pin this one.

### Pillar 2 — "The expensive way you're doing it now" [1–2 posts/week]
Face to camera, 30–45 seconds, one painful business habit and the cost of it. Your site already lists the four best ones: manual data entry between platforms, gut-feel decisions because reports take a day, hiring a person instead of building a system, and things falling through the cracks because follow-up depends on someone remembering.

Format: name the symptom → put a number on it → show the fix in three seconds of screen recording → "the build takes a week."

### Pillar 3 — "Behind the studio" [1 post/week]
The authenticity pillar, and the one most people skip because it feels unproductive. It isn't — it's what makes the other four believable.

Team meetups, a build that broke in production and what you did at 2am, a client message landing, what you got wrong on a scope, what running an automation studio out of Pakistan for international clients actually looks like day to day. Unpolished beats polished here.

### Pillar 4 — "Proof" [1 post/week]
Results and testimonials, but told as a story, never as a graphic with a star rating. You have unusually strong social proof — use it properly:
- The Upwork client whose messy manual reporting became real-time ("what used to take a full day now updates in real time").
- Tauseef Rehman, VP at Systems Limited, on the LinkedIn branding and outbound work.
- Shayan Malik, Industry Manager SMB at Meta.
- Eberly & Collard, a US PR agency.

Structure: the state before → what you built → the number after → a two-second flash of the actual testimonial. Your face delivering it, not a text card.

### Pillar 5 — "The take" [1 post/every other week]
Your opinion on AI, automation, and work. This is the pillar that gets shared outside your audience and the one that builds the personal brand rather than the pipeline. It's also where your existing thesis lives: in an age where AI can produce infinite content, the scarce asset is a real face attached to real work.

Related angles you're already credible on: why "AI agent" is mostly a rebrand of a workflow with an LLM at one decision point; why most companies should automate before they hire; why a $60K salary spent on copy-paste work is the most common invisible expense in a small business.

## 4. The LinkedIn → Instagram repurposing pipeline

You already have distribution on LinkedIn. Don't write twice — convert.

1. **Pick your top LinkedIn post each week** (by comments, not likes — comments mean it hit a nerve).
2. **Rewrite the hook for a spoken opening.** LinkedIn hooks are written to be read in a scroll; Instagram hooks must be *said* in the first two seconds. "Most agencies are lying about AI" reads fine on LinkedIn and dies on camera. "I've built 40 of these. Most of what you see labelled 'AI agent' isn't one." works.
3. **Strip the formatting.** No line-break poetry, no emoji bullets. Spoken language.
4. **Add the artifact.** Whatever the post claims, show the screen where it's true.
5. **Change the CTA.** LinkedIn CTA is "comment X". Instagram CTA is "DM me the word AUDIT" — because the DM is the channel, and you've literally built a DM automation.

Run this in reverse too: an Instagram Reel that performs becomes a LinkedIn text post the following week.

## 5. The CTA ladder

Three offers, escalating. Never more than one per post.

- **Soft (most posts):** "DM me AUDIT and I'll send you the 5-minute automation audit." Points at mincac.com/audit, which already scores and routes them — your funnel is built, the content just needs to feed it.
- **Medium (weekly):** The free build. "Five free builds a month. Tell me your worst manual process." This is the strongest offer you have and it is underused — it removes all risk and it gets you a case study.
- **Hard (every 8–10 posts):** "Book a call." Link in bio.

Ratio to hold: roughly 8 value posts to 1 direct pitch. The build-in-public content *is* the selling; the pitch is just permission to act.

## 7. Rules that keep it honest

- **Never post a build you didn't do.** The entire strategy rests on being verifiably real; one borrowed demo undoes it.
- **Show the failure at least monthly.** The broken node, the client who churned, the scope you underpriced. Nothing builds trust faster and nothing is easier to skip.
- **No AI voiceover, no AI avatar, no faceless edits.** You are betting on authenticity — hedging that bet is the only way to lose it.
- **Real numbers only.** You have enough genuine ones that inflating anything would be pure downside.
- **Batch on one day.** Record 5 Reels in one sitting, one outfit is fine, ship across the week. Consistency is the only variable that reliably matters at the start.
